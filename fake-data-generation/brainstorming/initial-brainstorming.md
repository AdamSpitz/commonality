# My initial thoughts, brainstorming ideas for examples of "network society" projects

Read [our thoughts on network societies](/docs/end-user/commonality/vision-and-strategy/credible-solution/network-societies.md), and anything else relevant.

Some thoughts on particular "network societies" that might be interesting to me:
  - "Crypto-friendly community": local education, advocacy, and lobbying, aimed at getting more local businesses to accept blockchain-based payments. e.g. Build and test an open-source checkout integration for one commonly used shop setup, including refunds and understandable receipts. Run an eight-week deployment trial with ten willing merchants and publish what worked, actual costs, and failures. Fund an independent security review of the shared integration. The reusable software and evidence are the public goods; subsidizing one shop's equipment is a different proposition. Note that this could be an organic alliance, supported by people coming from various perspectives: crypto-natives, people who don't trust the federal government for political reasons, business owners who are tired of paying 3% to the credit-card companies, etc.
  - "Improve assimilation": For people who are concerned about higher levels of immigration leading to immigrants who don't assimilate as well, we could have projects for various measures (implementable by either normal citizens or by local governments) to improve assimilation.
    - e.g.
      - Run twelve evening sessions of practical host-language practice: talking to a teacher, applying for work, explaining a maintenance problem, participating in a local meeting. (Provide childcare so parents can attend.)
      - Teach basic civics.
      - Publish a reusable instructor pack for doing this kind of teaching.
      - Pair newcomers and established residents for a season of repairing park facilities or maintaining a community garden: a shared task with a useful result, where conversation happens naturally.
      - Local-government policies: *Don't* offer indefinite bilingual service; create non-ethnic shared activities; use existing institutions (libraries, adult education, parks departments) as the main vehicles rather than creating parallel “immigrant offices” whose mission is primarily service delivery in origin languages; more even spatial distribution of public housing; model and enforce ordinary social norms in everyday life; etc.
    - Again, this is kind of an organic-alliance situation: maybe you're a right-winger who wants less immigration overall (and wants to at least assimilate the immigrants who do come), but you could also be someone who's fine with immigration but still believes it's a good thing when the immigrants assimilate, or you could even be an immigrant who simply wants to fit in better.
  - "End first-past-the-post": Push for using better voting systems than FPTP, aiming to try it in more-local elections first; could be interesting to try (non-binding, obviously) online polls that use other voting systems, to see what the outcomes would be; etc. Organic alliance angle: this might not even be particularly partisan (many on both right and left dislike FPTP).
  - "Let's stop fighting about abortion": Not so much about pushing for pro-life or pro-choice; more like "let's make the rules at the state level rather than the federal level, so that presidential elections and supreme-court appointments don't boil down to a referendum on abortion", plus maybe "let's advocate middle-of-the-road abortion policies like legal-until-12-weeks so that everyone is happy except the extremists on each side, then go on with our lives instead of fighting over this forever." Organic alliance angle: I think a lot of people on both left and right are tired of fighting about this.
  - "Low-carb society": form enclaves where the grocery stores and restaurants avoid carb-heavy junk food.
  - "Cancel-proof": supporting those who've been undeservedly "cancelled". Organic alliance angle: you don't actually have to be a right-winger to believe that cancel culture is wrong, you just have to be a liberal from 15 or 20 years ago.
  - "Common sense about gender": "Don't let kids chop off their genitals, and also don't be a jerk to people who are innately different." Organic alliance angle: you don't actually have to be a right-winger to believe that kids probably shouldn't be allowed to do gender transitions, you just have to be almost anyone at all whose beliefs were formed more than ten years ago.
  - "Unwoke": combination of the gender one and the cancellation one and various others.
  - "Democracy 2.0": could have organic-alliances with the anti-FPTP stuff above; using tools like Tally to do "voting" in a more fine-grained Internet-enabled way, and tools like Commonality to do public-goods-funding, rather than the legacy system where idiot corrupt representatives squabble and posture.
  - "AI isn't so important that we should be risking human extinction": make international treaties to let alignment research go ahead while slowing down capabilities research, etc.
  - "US-friendly Canadian": for Canadians who are perfectly happy to buy from the US, etc. This could be a bridge between various views: right-wingers who are generally fine with the US and Trump; normal everyday types who don't care that much about politics and just think it's silly to cut ties with the US when we obviously need them; people who have business interests or personal connections with the US; left-wingers who don't like Trump but take a longer-term view and expect the pendulum to swing; etc.
  - Christianity: Notice that Christianity itself is actually a pretty textbook example of a network state. They have the "network union" aspect (sticking up for each other). They have the "network archipelago" aspect (enclaves where believers live near each other, weekly meetups every Sunday, many other in-person gatherings too), they have the diplomatic-recognition aspect (tax-exempt status and special rules). And they could really use a public-goods-funding system like Commonality, because large-scale inter-church organizations/denominations are fraught with all sorts of annoying politics and squabbles over doctrine and so on. So... what kinds of projects would be helpful? Some thoughts, and I bet there's a lot more: "sticking up for each other" (when one has a problem that's too big for them, others pitch in to help); substitute preachers for a week when the pastor is sick or whatever; info, news, teaching materials, etc. Plus all sorts of other kinds of projects that they'd share with secular conservatives: pro-life efforts, etc. There's lots of potential for organic-alliances: between churches with the same doctrine, between churches of different doctrines who nevertheless have plenty of common beliefs; between Christians and secular conservatives; between Christians and Jews; etc.
  - Islamic reform: efforts to "soften" Islam so that it's less incompatible with the rest of the world.
  - Anti-regulation (or anti-specific-types-of-regulation, like Balaji's "FDA-free zone" idea)
  - Home-schooling: organizing local meetups of homeschoolers; teaching materials; planning local "field trips"; etc.
  - lots of others

(Some of those are just "advocate for this idea", which is a valid thing to do but it's also kinda lame and ineffectual; it's much more interesting when there are concrete things that can be done that might actually produce real value.)

Obviously this list is heavily biased towards my own political views. There's nothing about Commonality that would prevent people on the other side from using Commonality for their own stuff, but if *I'm* gonna try to jumpstart this thing with example projects, I'm going to choose causes that I think are good. (But it might be a good idea to keep these in a separate repo or something. Maybe we should split off the seed data stuff? OTOH, that feels silly.)

That said, I think there's potential for "bridges" ("organic alliances") here. This mostly isn't meant to be "let's make the right-wingers win", this is meant to be "I think there's a lot of common ground between sane common-sense people on both sides". Part of the job of running each of those causes is going to be writing bridges to get support from various separate potential constituencies. (Still, my preferences do generally exclude the radical left much more than the far right.)

This is a huge list of causes; I'm obviously not going to take those all on myself. But I bet that together we could come up with a decent list of *fake* examples illustrating all of these (including a few base-level projects for each one, and also meta-level projects for running each "network society"), in a way that'd make me (and anyone with roughly my political preferences) say "huh, cool, I can see how this system could be useful." The hope is that this inspires people to start doing them for real - "cause founder" types at first, who'll be motivated (out of a desire to further their cause, not out of a desire to expand usage of Commonality) to take on the job of finding people to do the various other jobs.

And the hope is that they'll notice not just "hey, those like good projects" but also "wow, I can see how this kind of thing might just *not happen* without this Commonality system". (That'll obviously be a rarer realization - lots of people *won't* think about it in those terms.)
  - "Huh, this cause-board is showing me a list of lots of aligned projects; I can see that these three in particular have momentum and could potentially reach their funding threshold. It's neat that I can get an overview of what this entire ecosystem is trying to accomplish, and where my money is most likely to be effective."
  - "Huh, I can have the cause-board show me only *already-funded but not-yet-reimbursed* projects; I'm much more comfortable directing my money to those and letting the less risk-averse types take on the task of guessing winners in advance."
  - "Huh, I can just fire-and-forget $20/month, to be directed not by a faceless org, but by my friend Joe."
  - etc.

What I want to do next is make a big fake-data campaign containing:
  - for each "society":
    - many statements, not just one; look for bridges where people coming from various perspectives would agree on this; make use of our statement-generation and bridge-creation guidelines to make these compelling and make the implication arrows actually work
      - also make some statements that are *incompatible* with these, because not all the fake users will agree with me on everything
      - and make the implication arrows if and only if the implication attestor actually agrees (I forget how we do that for these simulations, it doesn't need to be the real live implication attester, but you should at least run the statements through the real implication-attester prompt)
    - many fake crowdfundable projects
  - many fake users, with various combinations of roles and interests and various possible beliefs within each area, including beliefs that lead them *not* to sign or support these causes; have them sign the statements they'd sign, support some projects they'd support, etc.
  - whatever else is necessary to make this a proper realistic demo campaign (I'm sure I'm forgetting stuff)
