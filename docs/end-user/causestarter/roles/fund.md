# Provide money without running the cause

You can be useful simply by supplying capital. That is true for a $20 pledge and
for a whale-sized commitment. You do not have to start a foundation or find a
person who already combines money, expertise, reach, and operations.

## You do not also have to

- **Choose every project.** Delegate the allocation decisions to a person whose
  judgment you trust. Unspent funds remain revocable.
- **Judge technical claims yourself.** Scouts can find promising work; trusted
  evaluators can vouch for alignment and for results.
- **Manage the work or the people doing it.** Projects state a concrete deliverable
  and funding threshold. The doer owns execution.
- **Raise the rest of the goal.** With an [assurance
  contract](../../lazyGiving/assurance-contracts.md), your pledge is returned if
  the crowd does not reach the threshold.
- **Predict winners.** You can instead support work after it delivered by
  [reimbursing early contributors at cost](../../lazyGiving/retroactive-funding.md).

## How your money recruits the other roles

A visible pledge turns “someone should do this” into credible demand. Builders can
see that the work may actually be paid for. Scouts have a real budget to direct.
A large delegated commitment makes good allocators worth discovering, while the
public record of their choices makes it easier to decide whom to trust. Funding a
proven result replenishes early contributors so they can scout another attempt.

Capital therefore does more than pay the final bill: it creates a reason for
judgment, talent, and trusted connectors to gather around a concrete project.

## Start here

Open a cause’s **Pledges** page. Choose an amount and, if you do not want to pick
projects, [delegate](../../shared/key-ideas/delegation.md). To fund only after
seeing evidence, use the cause board’s **Not yet reimbursed** list.

See all the **[separable roles](./index.md)**.
