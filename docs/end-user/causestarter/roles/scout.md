# Spot promising work without supplying all the money

If you understand a field, notice capable people early, or enjoy finding neglected
projects, that judgment is a contribution of its own. You need not disguise it as
a large donation or turn it into a nonprofit.

## You do not also have to

- **Be wealthy.** Direct a modest early contribution, or become a delegate for
  people who have money but do not want to evaluate projects themselves.
- **Fund projects forever.** Fund as a scout and later donors may reimburse your
  contribution at cost, letting the same giving budget support another attempt.
- **Prove the completed result in advance.** Your job is prospective judgment.
  Other people can separately evaluate whether the project delivered.
- **Do the project.** Finding a capable doer and directing resources toward them is
  useful even when you cannot execute the work yourself.
- **Recruit a whole audience.** An alignment vouch from someone in the relevant
  trust network can place the project on cause boards people already follow.

## How your judgment recruits the other roles

A good early choice gives donors something specific to fund and gives doers a path
to start. Each visible choice becomes part of your track record. That track record
can persuade hands-off donors—including large ones—to delegate more money to you.
When the project succeeds, evaluators have evidence to inspect and later donors
have a proven result whose early contributors they can reimburse.

One scout can therefore convert undirected money into executable work, then make
both future capital and retrospective evaluation easier to attract.
