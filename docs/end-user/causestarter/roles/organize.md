# Organize a cause without building an all-purpose organization

The organizer’s job is to publish a useful invitation: the statements people can
support, the place projects can attach, and clear asks for the capacities still
missing. It is not to personally become the funder, expert, operator, evaluator,
and media department.

## You do not also have to

- **Own a canonical manifesto.** A board is a mix of independent statements.
  People can sign one, reject another, or publish a different mix that reuses the
  overlap.
- **Hold or allocate the money.** Donors can pledge directly; delegates can make
  allocation decisions in public.
- **Judge every proposal and result.** Recruit prospective scouts and retrospective
  evaluators separately.
- **Execute every project.** Publish the demand and let people with the relevant
  skills propose bounded work.
- **Already have every role staffed.** Begin with the capacity you have and use its
  visible output to make the next ask credible.

## Recruit a flywheel, not a roster

Start anywhere. Visible pledges tell builders there is demand. A concrete project
gives donors and scouts something to act on. A trusted delegate makes a large
hands-off pledge plausible. An alignment vouch distributes a project into existing
networks. Delivered work creates evidence for evaluators. Success vouches attract
later donors, whose reimbursements let early scouts fund the next attempt.

Ask each person for the narrow contribution that matches their leverage:

- “Would you pledge or delegate?”
- “Would you look for a credible project?”
- “Would you do this bounded piece of work?”
- “Would you vouch that this aligns—or later, that it delivered?”
- “Would you put this in front of people who trust you?”

A whale does not need to found the institution. A domain expert does not need to
bring the whale. Your board lets their separate yeses compound.
