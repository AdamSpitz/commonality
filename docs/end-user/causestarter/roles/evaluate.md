# Recognize results without predicting winners

Some people are much better at judging completed work than proposed work. You can
wait for evidence, inspect what happened, and contribute retrospective judgment or
money. You do not have to become an early-stage grant maker.

## You do not also have to

- **Choose from pitches.** Follow **Not yet reimbursed** and look at projects that
  already claim a result.
- **Supply money.** A trusted success vouch helps other people discover work worth
  funding even if you write no check.
- **Have performed the work.** Evaluate the evidence within the expertise and
  relationships you actually have.
- **Make a universal verdict.** A vouch is your public judgment. People who trust
  you can use it; there is no platform committee declaring official success.
- **Run the next project.** Later donors can reimburse early contributors, who can
  reuse that budget to find the next attempt.

## How your judgment recruits the other roles

Success vouches turn finished but obscure work into something trusted networks can
find. That gives cautious donors a concrete result to support. Their reimbursement
restores early scouts’ budgets, and a record of well-chosen successes makes those
scouts more credible to future delegates. Builders also gain proof that delivering
useful work can lead to recognition rather than disappearing after launch.

Retrospective judgment closes the loop: execution creates evidence, your vouch
makes the evidence legible, and later capital sends scouts back out to fund more
execution.

## Start here

Open a cause board’s **Not yet reimbursed** list. Inspect the result, vouch for
success if you can stand behind that judgment, or [fund it
retroactively](../../lazyGiving/retroactive-funding.md).
