# Unbundle the organization

People who care about a cause rarely lack motivation. What they usually lack is one
organization that has all of the things needed to act: money, judgment, trusted
connections, an audience, and people who can do the work.

That bundle is difficult to assemble. A conventional cause organization has to raise
money, choose a mission, recruit talent, evaluate proposals, administer grants, and
remain trusted enough to do all of those things on everyone else's behalf. A promising
effort can stall because no one person has the whole combination.

CauseStarter does not try to manufacture a following or replace the places where a
cause already gathers. It does something narrower: **it lets different people supply
those ingredients separately.**

- Someone with money can pledge without learning to evaluate every project.
- Someone with judgment can direct other people's pledges without first becoming a
  major donor or incorporating a foundation.
- Someone who understands past work can identify what actually delivered without
  taking responsibility for predicting the next winner.
- Someone trusted in the community can vouch for alignment or success without
  controlling a grant program.
- Someone capable of doing useful work can find visible demand without knowing the
  right grant officer.
- Someone with an audience can point followers to those jobs without turning the
  audience into members of a new organization.

The result is not an organization with its departments removed. It is a way for money,
judgment, trust, distribution, and skilled work to meet around individual statements
and projects when they are useful.

## One exceptional contribution is enough

Most funding systems recognize one kind of unusually powerful participant: a person
or institution with a great deal of money. But causes have several kinds of scarce
capacity:

- **Capital:** the ability to commit a large amount of money.
- **Judgment:** an unusually thorough understanding of what the ecosystem needs.
- **Execution:** the ability to deliver ambitious work reliably.
- **Trust:** a reputation whose vouches and funding decisions carry weight.
- **Distribution:** an audience of people who already care.

Traditionally, a new organization needs several of these to coincide. A funder needs a
mission and staff; an expert needs access to a budget; a builder needs fundraising and
connections. CauseStarter makes room for each exceptional contribution on its own. A
large funder does not have to invent a foundation, and the best-informed person does
not have to be wealthy for their judgment to direct substantial resources.

## Partial success recruits the missing roles

The pieces reinforce one another as soon as any of them appears:

- Visible pledged money tells builders that useful work can be funded.
- Visible projects tell donors that their money has somewhere concrete to go.
- A trusted delegate lets hands-off donors participate.
- A credible success voucher makes retroactive funding easier.
- A strong builder makes the funding opportunity more credible.
- A person with an audience can send each follower directly to the job that fits.

This changes how a cause gets started. The organizer does not need to recruit a whole
institution before anything is useful. They can recruit one contribution at a time,
and each contribution makes the pitch to the other roles more concrete: “There is
$1,000,000 available for useful work,” or “Here are projects that could put $1,000,000
to use.” Smaller contributions create the same flywheel at a smaller scale.

## Motivation still comes from the cause

CauseStarter is not a social network and does not promise to build an organizer's
audience. A community might gather on X, in churches, in professional networks, in a
neighborhood association, or somewhere else entirely. Those places create attention,
identity, and motivation.

CauseStarter becomes valuable after that: when someone already inclined to organize
asks how the people they know can turn their different kinds of capacity into action.
The organizer's pitch is not “please adopt this system.” It is “here is one useful
thing you can do for the cause you already care about, without taking on all the other
jobs.”

That is the everyday version of the larger idea: **[do the part you'd do
anyway](../../../causestarter/the-jobs.md)**. For the organizer's version, see
**[Start a cause](../../../causestarter/start-a-cause.md)**.
