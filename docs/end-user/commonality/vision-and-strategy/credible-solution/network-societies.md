# Balaji-style "Network Societies"

# My initial question

Imagine something like a Balaji-style Network Society. (For whatever purpose: the "sugar-free" one, or "FDA-free" one, or "unwoke" one, or whatever.) What kinds of work would be involved in "running" something like that? I mean, there's the base-level meat of whatever the society is *for*. But then there's the meta-level stuff, which I imagine is pretty similar between societies. (Still needs to be *done* per-society, but the *type* of work is similar.)

e.g.
  - Maintaining the society's website.
  - Running local events, to let people meet like-minded local people.
  - Running larger-scale events, drawing people from a wider area.
  - Everyday behind-the-scenes basic stuff like "Alice, who is well-connected, helps point people to other like-minded people near them."
  - etc.

(My reason for asking: the original purpose of this entire Commonality project was to try to be an implementation of something sorta like Balaji-style network societies, but with the underlying infrastructure done in a more-sophisticated way, not so much like the "big Discord with a treasury" that I think Balaji was imagining. So now that we've got a bunch of infrastructure implemented, I want to think about what kinds of use cases we might come up with that are along the lines of "fund the kinds of work that this network-society needs".)

(ADAM: I've put in a bunch of notes below, mostly about the way I'm imagining this differently than I think Balaji does. I'm imagining a more-decentralized thing, with less need for coordination; a bunch of the point of Commonality is about making it possible to get similar "we can work together" results without needing to coordinate on a single group. So my goal here is twofold: lay out the vision for how to achieve something sorta like the Network Society idea but in this different way; and also identify kinds of work along those lines that might be worth funding using this system. That is: could we put together a playbook, e.g. a set of example LazyGiving projects, that would in-aggregate constitute something sort-of like "hey we're now crowdfunding the work needed to make something that looks like a functioning Network Society"?)


# AI's answer

The meta-work is what turns a shared preference into a society that still exists next year. The purpose (sugar, medicine, ideology) changes the directory and the events. The jobs underneath stay the same: a front door, a way to find each other, gatherings at a few scales, a memory, a trust system, people who keep the local nodes alive, and a small interface to the outside world. (ADAM: yes.)

## Front door

Someone has to say what this society is, in one place a stranger can find.

* **Canon.** The one-commandment, the boundaries, and a short account of why those boundaries exist. It has to be revised on purpose, or it drifts into whatever the loudest chapter is doing. (ADAM: This is one area where I think Commonality can do *better* than that. A big part of the point of Commonality is enabling *many* "one commandments" - anyone can write/sign whatever statements they want, and have a cause board centred on projects aligned with whatever statements they want. There's definitely still a place for leaders who can write good cause statements and so on, so this job you're describing still does exist; but I think Commonality is architected in a way that should require less of the "let's all coordinate on this one statement written by this one leader" that the original network-society vision requires.)
* **The site.** Not a brochure. The live map of chapters, the next events, how to join, and where the canon lives. (ADAM: Again, I'm imagining something requiring less coordination: less like "here's *the* map of chapters", more like "anyone can publish their own map of chapters", as well as "any chapter can publish their own existence if they want to; this can then be automatically discovered by tools that show a directory". Doesn't change the need for the job; I'm just trying to reimagine this as something needing less coordination.)
* **Onboarding.** A path from "I read a post" to "I know what is expected of me and who to talk to in my city." That includes rejecting people who want the aesthetic and not the practice.

## Finding each other

This is the Alice job, and it does not scale by adding more Alices unless the introductions are written down.

* **Local matching.** Pointing a new person at the three members near them who will actually reply.
* **A directory of people and providers.** Doctors, restaurants, tutors, employers, hosts, landlords — whoever counts as "one of ours." The list rots in months unless someone is paid to check it.
* **Routing.** "Who do I ask about X" inside the society. Most of this is private judgment, and the valuable artifact is the judgment staying available after Alice is busy.

## Gatherings

Events are how a chat becomes a population.

* **Local recurring meetings.** A host, a room, a rhythm, and enough facilitation that newcomers are not stranded. The hard part is the sixth month, when the host is tired.
* **Regional gatherings.** Worth traveling for. Programming, ticketing, housing, and a reason to come that is not "meet strangers who agree with us."
* **The big event.** Once a year, society-scale. This is also where norms get demonstrated in public, so the program and the code of conduct are the product.
* **Pop-up presence.** A street, a building, or a week in one city where members actually live together. Balaji's archipelago is this job repeated until it has an address.

(ADAM: yes.)

## Memory and voice

* **Internal record.** What was decided, which chapters are alive, which experiments failed. A wiki with an editor, not a Discord scrollback.
* **Outbound explanation.** Newsletter, talks, a standing answer to hostile questions. One voice is enough; several contradictory ones read as no society. (ADAM: I'd quibble with this a bit. I agree that the less-centralized thing I'm imagining reads as being less unified, but I'd say that's just the nature of humanity; we're *not* actually going to agree on everything, and the point of a lot of Commonality - see the "organic coalitions" stuff - is to enable us to work together when we do agree on *something* rather than the status quo where we often can't work together unless we agree on *everything*. As usual, I'm not saying the job of writing outbound-explanations shouldn't exist; it's just that there'll be lots of people doing it and doing it differently and that's fine.)
* **Translation and local adaptation.** The same commandment has to be usable in another language and another city without each chapter inventing a new society. (ADAM: Right, so in my vision "inventing a new society" isn't really a thing, or isn't really a problem - I mean, translate the commandment if you want, or rewrite whatever you want in your own language, doesn't matter too much.)

## Trust

Every values community attracts people extracting status, money, or cover. The meta-work is a reputation system that is legible across chapters.

* **Vouching.** Who may introduce a new member, and what they are on the hook for.
* **Dispute handling.** Two members in conflict need a person and a procedure, or the conflict becomes the society's main activity.
* **Removal.** A way to say someone is no longer in, that other chapters will respect. This is the job that starts to look like government. It still has to be staffed. (ADAM: In my vision, the idea is more like: local chapters are small groups that need this kind of "we can kick you out" ability; above that scale it's more like "I'll unfollow you on X". There's no central discussion group that needs moderation. And there's no central treasury to fight for control over - that's the big benefit of the assurance-contract ecosystem idea.)
* **Scam and quality control on the directory.** A listed clinic, school, or host that embarrasses the society is a society failure, not a listing failure.

## Keeping the doers

The society dies when the local host burns out and nobody knows the playbook.

* **Host and chapter kits.** How to start a city: first ten people, first venue, first three months, when to stop.
* **Recruiting the next host** before the current one quits.
* **Succession** for the founder-shaped roles: canon editor, main event lead, the person outsiders call.

(ADAM: Yes.)

## Census

Balaji's version is an auditable count of people, income, and real estate, because a society that cannot count itself cannot bargain. Even without diplomatic ambitions, someone has to know which chapters are real.

* **Headcount that is hard to fake.** Attendance, dues, or some other act, not Discord joins. (ADAM: I think both Tally and LazyGiving will be useful for this.)
* **Liveness.** Which cities met this month. Which providers are still practicing.
* **Outcome numbers for the purpose.** Sugar metrics, case series, school results. The measurement method is meta-work; the activity being measured is the meat.

## Interface with the outside

The society is not a country. Its events, houses, and clinics still sit inside one.

* **Legal wrappers.** An entity that can sign a venue contract, buy insurance, and hold a lease in each country where people gather.
* **Permits, insurance, safety** for anything bigger than a dinner.
* **Deals.** Employers, schools, clinics, landlords, and adjacent societies. Someone has to negotiate and then tell the directory.
* **Crisis logistics.** A member is stranded, doxxed, or sick in a city where the parallel service exists. The valuable public good is the response path, not each private transfer.

(ADAM: Yes.)

## What is actually fundable here

A lot of this splits cleanly into work you already have language for.

| Kind of meta-work | Shape | Fits an assurance contract when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The site, the canon revision, the host kit, the directory rebuild | A lump of work with a visible artifact | Partial funding buys nothing; the artifact is public |
| A local meeting series, a regional weekend, the annual event | Hosting an encounter | The gathering happens only if the threshold is met |
| Checking the directory, reproducing a provider's claims, counting a chapter | Producing evidence | The check is public and the checker is not the person being checked |
| Alice's introductions, mediation, the standing on-call host | A person's ongoing judgment | The month of availability is the work item, the way a maintenance month is |
| "Someone should feel welcomed" | Continuous, rival, private | A bad fit. Fund the role or the event, not the feeling |

The roles that feel most like "just being a good member" (Alice, the local host, the canon editor) are the ones a treasury-Discord usually fills with unpaid status. They are also the ones that determine whether the society is real. Describing them as named, repeating work items — this city's host for this quarter, this directory audit, this gathering — is the funding use case. The purpose-specific meat (the sugar program, the clinic, the school) then hangs off a society that can still introduce two members in the same city.
