# Getting stuff done effectively

Problem: Even when money is available, getting it to the people who'll actually deliver results — rather than those best at navigating the funding process — is hard.

Government's solution: procurement processes, RFPs, grant review panels. Selects for grant-writing skill and political connections, not project quality. Enormous overhead: compliance reporting, administrative layers, political conditions. Estimates of 30-50% overhead for certain categories are common.

Commonality's solution: retroactive funding. Instead of trying to predict which proposals will succeed, later donors can support projects that have *already* demonstrated results by reimbursing their early contributors at cost. This favors demonstrated execution over polished proposal-writing and lets scouts reuse the same giving budget. It does not give anyone interest, a premium, or a profit. Not always applicable (some projects still need early contributors willing to front the money), but where it works it should produce better outcomes. And assurance contracts have far less overhead than government procurement.

For the full argument on retroactive funding, see [retroactive-funding](../why-its-better/retroactive-funding.md).
