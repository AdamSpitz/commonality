# Delegation solves the laziness problem

Problem: Most people don't have the time or expertise to evaluate every cause themselves, so good causes go underfunded simply because nobody's paying attention.

Government's solution: representative democracy. Elect someone and hope they spend your tax money well. Works okay, but you get one representative for all issues, for a fixed term, with no granularity and limited accountability.

Commonality's solution: fine-grained delegation. Pick your own delegate(s), per-cause, revocable at any time. You can be just as lazy as you are in a democracy — delegate everything and forget about it — but with better matching (your delegate specializes in the topic you care about) and instant accountability (revoke if they do something you don't like). No coordination problem of "who should we all delegate to" — just delegate to whoever you individually trust.

Delegation is **one hop**. Your delegate cannot pass your spending authority onward to someone you never chose. If they know a better fit, they can suggest you delegate to that person — but the choice stays yours. This is deliberate: chains of delegation are how "someone I personally chose" quietly becomes "whoever a stranger picked," which is the traditional-charity problem all over again.

It's also the design's central trade: Commonality encourages the crowd to centralize by **exactly one level** — to specialists who follow the ecosystem and make funding decisions for a living or a hobby — but no further. That one level buys most of the efficiency of professional grant-making without losing the personal relationship that makes delegation trustworthy: your delegate is someone you know or whose public record you've actually looked at, not a faceless org or a politician. But because decision-making is still spread across a large, open-ended pool of personally-chosen delegates — not concentrated in one big org's treasury — the underlying system still has to be crowdfunding-shaped: assurance contracts that pool money from many donors. You can't replace it with a top-down grant machine, because there is no single machine to be top of.

This is part of the broader [individualization](../why-its-better/individualization.md) advantage: preserving individual preferences further into the pipeline produces better collective outcomes.
