# Activate a cause one role at a time

This playbook is for **cause organizers**: people who publish a mix of statements
and recruit jobs onto that board. A **vertical operator** (the founder who owns a
branded front end, policies, and distribution) is a different role. One person may
fill both, but they do not collapse. The operator still owns distribution; the
organizer still recruits one useful contribution at a time. See
[ADR 0009](/specs/decisions/0009-causes-are-publications-over-statements.md).

CauseStarter does not create motivation or build an audience for a cause. Use the
places where your people already gather: social media, professional networks, local
groups, churches, newsletters, conferences, or existing organizations.

What CauseStarter gives you is a much easier answer to the next question: **how can
those already-motivated people cooperate?**

You do not have to recruit an organization that combines money, prospective
judgment, retrospective judgment, execution, trust, and distribution. Choosing a
mission is what publishing statements already does; it is not a sixth person to
hire. Recruit one useful contribution at a time. The system makes those
contributions visible and lets them connect.

## Start with the people you already know

If you are inclined to organize this cause, you probably already know the names of
some of its unusually capable people. Make a short map:

| Capacity | Who might have it? | The ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Money | Major donors, companies, foundations, many small donors | Pledge without also running a grant program |
| Prospective judgment | Domain experts who spot promising work early | Scout or direct initial funding |
| Retrospective judgment | People who can verify what actually delivered | Vouch for success and surface work for reimbursement |
| Execution | Builders, researchers, writers, operators | Publish a project against visible demand |
| Trust | Respected connectors in the community | Vouch for alignment or success so work reaches their network |
| Distribution | Influencers, newsletters, community leaders | Send each follower to the role that fits them |

One person may fill several rows, but your plan should not require that. The point is
to make an ask that fits the capacity they already have.

## Invite jobs, not members

Do not lead with “join our platform” or ask everyone to understand the entire system.
Send each person a self-contained invitation:

- **Hands-off donor:** “If you would put in $20 a month but do not want another
  responsibility, pledge it and delegate the project choices to someone you trust.”
- **Large funder:** “You can make substantial capital available without inventing a
  mission, staffing a foundation, or personally selecting every recipient.”
- **Prospective scout:** “You know this field well enough to spot promising work early.
  Fund it yourself or direct delegated funds; your record can earn greater trust.”
- **Retrospective evaluator:** “You do not have to predict winners. Find work that
  demonstrably delivered, vouch for its success, and help later donors reimburse its
  early contributors at cost.”
- **Builder:** “There is money looking for work that advances this cause. Publish the
  project and get it vouched onto a statement people already watch; you do not need an
  introduction to a grant committee.”
- **Trusted connector:** “Your vouch can put worthwhile work in front of everyone whose
  trust network reaches you. You do not have to fund or perform the work.”
- **Person with an audience:** “Point followers to a clear menu of useful jobs. Each can
  do one thing without joining an organization or inheriting the other roles.”

The public catalog to link is **[Do the part you'd do
anyway](/docs/end-user/causestarter/the-jobs.md)**. When possible, quote or link the
specific role instead of making a newcomer read every conceptual page first.

## Bootstrap from whichever side appears first

An empty board is an abstract promise. Any real contribution gives you a more concrete
next pitch:

- If pledges appear first: **“There is $X available for projects serving this cause.”**
- If projects appear first: **“Here is work that could put $X to good use.”**
- If a respected delegate appears first: **“You can contribute without personally
  evaluating every proposal.”**
- If successful work appears first: **“Here is a result you can inspect before giving.”**
- If a trusted voucher appears first: **“Projects can reach this community without
  winning over a central committee.”**
- If an audience appears first: give its members the role menu and see which capacity
  becomes visible.

Do not wait for a perfect launch containing all of them. Each role creates demand for
the others. Small contributors prove the loop; an unusually large contribution in any
one role can accelerate it dramatically.

## Show supply and demand plainly

The board should help a visitor answer two questions immediately:

- If I might build something, how much money is looking for useful work here?
- If I might pledge, what projects could put my money to use?

Use concrete numbers and projects in outreach as soon as they exist. “A new funding
protocol” is abstract. “$80,000 is available for Ethereum client diversity, and these
three teams are looking for funding” is a reason to act.

The same principle applies to nonfinancial capacity. Name the respected delegates,
show who vouched that a project delivered, and make successful work legible. Visible
capacity is recruitment material for the missing capacity.

## Keep the boundary honest

You still own distribution. CauseStarter will not create a following, decide which
influencers matter, or persuade people to care about the cause. It narrows your
coordination problem after you reach them.

That is a substantial reduction. Instead of asking, “Who can build and legitimate a
complete new organization?”, you can ask:

> Who would do one useful part for this cause if doing so did not saddle them with all
> the other parts?
